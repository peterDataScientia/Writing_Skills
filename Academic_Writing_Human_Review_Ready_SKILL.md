# Academic Writing Skill: Human-Reviewed, Submission-Ready Voice

## 1. Core standard

Write the **final scholarly work**, not a dialogue about whether the work is good enough. The author presents a coherent, defensible study for scholarly assessment. A reviewer—not the manuscript's narrator—decides whether the contribution merits publication. This does **not** mean hiding genuine limitations, manipulating results, or claiming verification that has not occurred. It means separating real scholarly qualifications from speculative AI-invented objections.

**Operating rule:** Every qualification, limitation, uncertainty statement, and methodological caveat must have a specific evidentiary or methodological basis, a clear effect on interpretation, and an appropriate location. Remove statements whose only function is to pre-empt hypothetical reviewer criticism.

## 2. Modes: keep private editing separate from public scholarship

**A. Internal audit mode (private working notes)**
- Detect discrepancies, missing files, unverified calculations, potential reporting omissions, and citation issues.
- Classify each issue as `VERIFIED PROBLEM`, `UNVERIFIED—CHECK`, `RESOLVED`, or `NOT A PROBLEM`.
- Point to the exact section, source, or dataset that establishes the issue.
- Do not silently transfer audit notes into the article.
- Do not declare that checks have been completed unless actual records, outputs, and files substantiate that claim.

**B. Manuscript mode (what readers see)**
- Report only confirmed design, data, methods, findings, and relevant limitations.
- Use declarative scientific language and completed-work reporting (past tense for methods/results).
- Avoid self-assessment such as “this study is robust,” “the review is fully compliant,” or “all concerns were addressed.”
- Do not include workflow status, internal doubts, draft history, chat instructions, or appeals for reviewer leniency.

**C. Author response mode (only if responding to peer review)**
- Acknowledge specific reviewer observations; identify corrections and their location.
- Do not pre-write imagined reviewer objections into the manuscript.

## 3. The evidence gate for negative or doubtful claims

Before including a sentence such as “the search cannot be independently verified,” “screening decisions remain uncertain,” or “the synthesis may be unreliable,” answer all four questions:

1. **What exact evidence supports the claim?** Identify the particular missing record, failed check, conflict, or design constraint.
2. **Is this still true in the FINAL version?** Verify whether the manuscript, supplements, repository, or completed audit already resolves it.
3. **Does it matter for interpreting the study?** Identify the scientific consequence, not a hypothetical referee reaction.
4. **Is this the right place?** A genuine remaining limitation belongs in a proportionate limitations/discussion passage; a fixable documentation error belongs in the internal task list until corrected.

If the evidence gate fails, **delete or rewrite the claim**; do not label a completed workflow “uncertain” merely because external readers have not yet inspected it.

## 4. Prohibited AI-style manuscript behaviors

Unless directly supported by the actual research, do **not**:

- Invent missing screening records, unverified appraisal judgments, absent supplements, unvalidated figures, or bibliographic errors.
- Reopen a finalized analysis simply because an AI assistant previously suggested it should be checked.
- Call an eligible compound “an exception,” “reference-inhibitor limitation,” or “exploratory observation” solely because its use in the original study differed from that of other compounds.
- Add “some decisions could not be independently verified” when complete screening and exclusion records exist.
- Write “further validation is required” as a generic final sentence when the study's actual objective is methodological synthesis rather than experimental validation.
- Advertise uncertainty twice—once in Results and again in each Discussion paragraph—without new analytical meaning.
- Describe absent novelty (“no one has ever studied X”) without a defensible literature basis.
- Insert hedges such as “possibly,” “perhaps,” “it could be argued,” “appears to suggest,” or “may potentially” by habit.
- Write defensive paragraphs explaining why the research was performed despite perceived shortcomings.
- Mention drafts, prior errors, model outputs, conversations, pending tasks, or “reviewer flags” in the manuscript.
- Misrepresent a source, fabricate verification, suppress contrary findings, conceal conflicts of interest, or omit genuine material limitations.

## 5. Precision over defensive language

The goal is calibrated authority, **not absolute certainty**. Replace vague anxiety with the exact statistical or methodological fact.

| Avoid | Prefer, when supported |
|---|---|
| “These results may not be reliable due to possible study differences.” | “Effect estimates varied with study weighting, as shown by the prespecified sensitivity analysis.” |
| “Screening decisions may not be independently verifiable.” | “Study selection and reasons for full-text exclusion are documented in Supplementary Table S2.” *(Only when S2 actually contains them.)* |
| “The reference inhibitor could limit the validity of the analysis.” | “The compound met the eligibility criteria and was included as a matched observation.” |
| “There is considerable uncertainty about the findings.” | “The 95% confidence interval crossed the null value, precluding a directional conclusion for the pooled estimate.” |
| “To our knowledge, no prior study has ever investigated this.” | “Existing studies addressed [defined question]; the present analysis examines [specific unresolved comparison].” |
| “The study might potentially offer useful insights.” | “The analysis quantifies [defined relationship] using [dataset and method].” |
| “All methods were thoroughly validated and are flawless.” | “Model performance was evaluated using [specified validation procedure and metric].” |
| “A reviewer could question the sample composition.” | “One study contributed 29 of 46 observations; exclusion of that study changed the observation-weighted ratio from [A] to [B].” |

**Important:** A confidence interval encompassing the null, small study numbers, high heterogeneity, selection bias, missing outcome data, and unvalidated predictions can be material scientific limitations. Do not disguise them. State exactly what the evidence supports, neither more nor less.

## 6. Distinguish true limitations from ordinary design decisions

For each proposed limitation, classify:

- **Material limitation:** A verified aspect of design/data/analysis that limits the scope, strength, or generality of inference. **Report it** accurately.
- **Resolved documentation issue:** A once-missing appendix, screening decision, or cross-reference now supplied. **Remove obsolete wording** and ensure the cited material exists.
- **Eligible methodological choice:** An inclusion decision that conforms to stated criteria. **Describe it neutrally**, not as a limitation without evidence of impact.
- **Unsupported speculative criticism:** A hypothetical reviewer concern with no identified evidence. **Exclude it** from the manuscript.
- **Remediable error:** Inconsistent counts, missing labels, incorrect DOI, unmatched units, broken figure reference. **Correct the file**, then remove draft-warning language.

Do not conflate “the reviewer can ask about this” with “this is a scientifically established limitation.”

## 7. Systematic review and evidence-synthesis rules

1. Report actual databases, search dates, strategies, screening process, eligibility criteria, included studies, extracted observations, and analysis as performed.
2. Ensure all PRISMA flow counts reconcile arithmetically and with the screening ledger. Reference the correct checklist and flowchart, if available.
3. Distinguish **studies**, **reports**, **compounds**, **assays**, and **compound–study observations**. Never substitute one count for another.
4. Include an observation if it meets prespecified eligibility criteria, regardless of whether the original article called it a control, reference inhibitor, or test compound. Preserve any scientifically relevant distinction only when justified.
5. Describe evidence appraisal as conducted, including framework and domains, without claiming all judgments were “independently confirmed” unless records support this.
6. Describe heterogeneity in terms of observed variation, known assay differences, and analytical sensitivity; avoid attributing causality without evidence.
7. Report statistical estimates, intervals, sensitivity analyses, exclusions, and denominators accurately. Preserve the difference between correlation, agreement, calibration, and causal inference.
8. If a source record is genuinely missing or irreproducible, do not erase that fact. Resolve it if possible; otherwise disclose its actual effect proportionately.
9. Reference supplementary materials by their existing contents. Do not imply S1–S6 are available unless they exist and match the submitted files.
10. The Discussion should interpret results in relation to the research question, not become an unsolicited audit of the review's legitimacy.

## 8. Paragraph-level writing discipline

For each paragraph:

- Start with one precise scientific point, supported by a result, study, or established concept.
- Explain the result or its mechanism with evidence; do not inflate its significance.
- Connect it naturally to the next paragraph and the study objective.
- Use active or clear passive constructions rather than defensive verbal padding.
- Cite sources for substantive external claims; keep numerical statements consistent with tables and figures.
- Avoid repetitive formulae (“It is important to note,” “A key limitation is,” “It should be acknowledged”) unless they serve a real argument.
- Prefer plain, discipline-appropriate language over inflated phrases (“highly novel,” “groundbreaking,” “comprehensive and rigorous” without defined support).
- State conclusions proportional to the data; a manuscript need not apologize for its scope.

### Tense guide
- Established knowledge: present tense (“Urease catalyzes ...”).
- Completed methods: past tense (“Records were screened ...”).
- Reported results: past tense (“Forty-six observations were included ...”).
- Interpretation/conclusion: present tense where appropriate (“These findings distinguish ...”).

## 9. Final submission-facing quality gate

Run the checks **privately**, then produce clean text. Every check must refer to the current final artifacts—not remembered claims about earlier versions.

### Scientific accuracy
- [ ] Research question and outcome are explicit and matched to the analysis.
- [ ] Every numerical claim agrees with the final dataset, code output, tables, abstract, and conclusion.
- [ ] Results distinguish statistical significance, effect magnitude, uncertainty, and practical implications.
- [ ] Contradictory or null findings are not omitted or euphemized.
- [ ] Actual design constraints and material limitations are described proportionately.

### Documentation integrity
- [ ] Eligibility decisions follow stated criteria.
- [ ] Screening counts, flowchart, exclusion reasons, and supplements agree.
- [ ] Evidence appraisal claims correspond to actual appraisal records.
- [ ] Figure/table numbering, captions, citations, DOI details, and file links are correct.
- [ ] Computational methods, software, versions, seeds, and code availability are stated only as verified.

### Writing integrity
- [ ] No invented uncertainty, generic self-doubt, or speculative “reviewer flags” remain.
- [ ] No obsolete pending-verification or unfinished-work statements remain after tasks are completed.
- [ ] No eligible study/compound is framed as a deficiency solely because of its label.
- [ ] No internal audit notes or AI conversational traces appear in the manuscript.
- [ ] Conclusions are confident but do not exceed the evidence.
- [ ] Genuine material limitations, ethics, funding, conflicts, and journal-mandated disclosures remain intact.

### Delivery
- [ ] Save the revised final document; preserve underlying data and reported findings unless a justified correction is made.
- [ ] Provide a concise separate change log, not inserted into the article.
- [ ] If a check cannot be performed, disclose that **in the handoff note**, not as an invented scientific limitation inside the manuscript; if its absence materially affects interpretation or required reporting, handle it transparently in the manuscript too.

## 10. Example: repairing inappropriate uncertainty

**Problematic draft:**
> Incomplete documentation of some screening decisions limits independent verification of review procedures.

**When complete records genuinely exist:**
> Study selection procedures and reasons for exclusion at full-text screening are provided in the PRISMA flow diagram and Supplementary Table S2.

**When records do not exist:** Do not claim that they exist. Recover them if possible; otherwise describe the actual documentation gap and any consequence for reproducibility. Never remove a true reporting deficiency solely to make the study sound complete.

**Problematic draft:**
> Inclusion of an acetohydroxamic acid reference inhibitor represents an additional limitation of the dataset.

**When it satisfies eligibility rules:**
> The matched acetohydroxamic acid observation was retained because enzyme-specific inhibitory concentrations were reported for both ureases in the same study.

Do not assign a bias or limitation to a compound merely because the originating paper used it as a positive control. Consider study-level influence empirically when relevant.

## 11. General reusable prompt for an AI writing assistant

> Edit this academic manuscript as a submission-facing scholarly work. Keep internal audit findings separate from manuscript prose. Remove gratuitous self-doubt, speculative reviewer objections, AI-generated warning statements, obsolete “pending verification” language, repetitive hedging, and unsupported limitations. Verify each proposed concern against the actual final data, methods, citations, and supplements before retaining it. Present completed work in clear, confident, evidence-calibrated language. Preserve all genuine material limitations, non-significant findings, uncertainty intervals, ethics and conflict disclosures, and reporting requirements. Do not invent checks, evidence, statistics, references, or completeness. Change only what the evidence supports. Return the clean manuscript and a separate factual change log.

## 12. Failure tests (the skill fails if any are true)

- It claims full PRISMA compliance without matching actual records.
- It asserts screening is incomplete solely because the AI did not inspect an already completed appendix.
- It removes a valid confidence interval, bias concern, or important limitation to project certainty.
- It invents literature novelty, verification, or experimental evidence.
- It treats an eligible positive-control compound as ineligible without a methodological basis.
- It lets internal “reviewer might flag” phrasing leak into submission prose.
- It mistakes confident presentation for unwarranted certainty.

**Final principle:** Present the strongest defensible account of completed scholarship. Be clear and assured about what the work establishes; be exact about what it cannot establish. Never manufacture doubt—and never manufacture certainty.
