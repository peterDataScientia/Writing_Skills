# Inference Boundaries, Assumptions and Validation

Ask **what exact claim the proposed evidence can warrant**.

## Levels of scientific inference
- **Description:** what is observed in a defined sample.
- **Association:** statistical relationship, potentially confounded.
- **Prediction:** expected out-of-sample behaviour under a specified data-generating setting.
- **Mechanism:** explanation of the processes producing the phenomenon, requiring appropriate independent support.
- **Causation:** claims about intervention effects, requiring a defensible identification strategy or experiment.

Do not upgrade one level to another without evidence.

## Operational tests
**Inference limitation:** identify the exact conclusion blocked, the evidence currently available, why it cannot establish the conclusion, consequences and a discriminating next investigation.

**Consequential omission:** establish how an omitted variable, process, context or assumption might alter the estimand, prediction or decision; theoretical plausibility alone does not prove an effect.

**Construct and endpoint equivalence:** compare definitions, measures, context, populations and timescales before claiming that evidence is missing or comparable.

**Assumption validity:** name the assumption; identify why it matters, when it may fail, how to test it and how failure changes the conclusion.

**Trade-offs:** state competing requirements and relevant metrics (e.g. speed versus physical fidelity), not an unevidenced assertion that a trade-off is unavoidable.

**Performance versus reliability:** distinguish internal validation performance from calibrated uncertainty, robustness, domain shift, external applicability or deployment success.

**Problem quantification:** provide sourced magnitude, denominator, comparator and context where meaningful; not every conceptual problem needs an arbitrary statistic.

## Computational and chemical examples
- Docking pose/score is not biochemical inhibition.
- BRD4 BD1-versus-BD2 activity is not BRD4-L-versus-BRD4-S isoform specificity.
- Internal cross-validation does not establish reliability in shifted chemical space.
- Model descriptors associated with an outcome do not establish underlying physical mechanism.
- Experimentally measured post-process predictors may not be available at design time.
All examples are conditional illustrations; verify domain-specific claims against actual evidence before incorporation into a manuscript.
