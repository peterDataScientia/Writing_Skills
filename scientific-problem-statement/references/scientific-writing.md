# Scientific Problem Statement Writing and Revision

## Write as a scientific argument
Use precise, economical, connected academic prose. Prefer an explicit research question and consequence over theatrical claims of novelty. Preserve the user's requested paragraph count, section numbering, language variant, citation style and original scientific scope.

## Composition
1. Establish relevant prior findings (not general textbook background).
2. Compare directly relevant evidence and its demonstrated boundaries.
3. Explain the unresolved inference and why current evidence cannot close it.
4. State the scientific/practical consequences without overstating them.
5. Justify an appropriately bounded, answerable investigation.

This need not be five paragraphs. One strong paragraph can contain the whole argument.

## Three writing techniques
**Concession–limitation:** acknowledge what prior studies achieved before showing a demonstrated limitation.

**Comparative synthesis density:** make paragraphs yield analytical conclusions, not a succession of paper descriptions. Density means meaning per comparison, not jargon or longer sentences.

**Problem–evidence–method traceability:** every major investigative question should trace to a documented limitation and a feasible observation, method or comparison; do not invent a gap to justify a preselected method.

## Editing discipline
- Preserve essential findings, references, numbers, variables and intended inference.
- Refine claim strength before changing scope or introducing new methods.
- Avoid phrases such as "there are no studies," "proves," "first ever" unless established.
- Do not remove nuance just to compress a paragraph.
- Do not make the problem a list of objectives or a mini-methods chapter.
- Maintain paragraph-to-paragraph logical progression and eliminate redundant motivation.
- Distinguish scientific problems from practical inconvenience and method availability.

## Paragraph-level silent audit
For each paragraph: what is its argumentative function? Which source supports its pivotal claim? Is the comparison logically fair? Is the level of inference calibrated? Does it advance the argument? Could a reader grasp the scientific uncertainty if algorithm/software names were removed?

## When revising supplied text
Return the complete revised passage when asked, and briefly flag substantive—not cosmetic—scientific changes. If a major premise lacks evidence, disclose this rather than silently invent support.
