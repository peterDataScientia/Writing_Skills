# Evidence-Based Research Gap Verification

## Goal
Establish a **consequential unresolved scientific question**, rather than fabricate novelty around a technique.

## Gap verification protocol
1. Formulate one provisional gap as a falsifiable question: "Can existing evidence already answer X, under conditions Y?"
2. Record search date, sources/databases used, exact search terms and synonyms, and the scope of coverage. State what could not be accessed.
3. Search for direct prior attempts and for counterexamples that could **disprove** the gap, including recent and non-obvious papers.
4. Compare outcome definitions, population/chemical/physical domain, methods, validation, and conclusions of relevant studies.
5. Identify the missing inference, not just the missing procedure or item.
6. Explain why this uncertainty matters: scientific interpretation, decision, prediction, policy, or design consequence.
7. Match a feasible investigation, data and validation strategy to the uncertainty.
8. Use bounded wording; where search is incomplete, report the gap as provisional.

## Recognisable gap families
Knowledge, measurement, methodological, validation, theory/assumption, contextual/transportability, evaluation and identification gaps. These may overlap; do not force mutually exclusive categories.

## Reject weak gaps
- "No one has used algorithm X" unless the algorithm's absence creates a consequential inference limitation.
- "Few studies in country Y" without a justified reason for context-dependent differences.
- "Structure/dataset absent" without saying what scientific inference is blocked.
- "High R²; further work needed" with no identified limitation of evaluation or application.
- "Previous research is inadequate" without comparing the specific achievements and restrictions.

## Output standard
What is known; what cannot be inferred; evidence and counterevidence; why the limit matters; what an appropriate study could establish; confidence/coverage limitations of the literature search.
