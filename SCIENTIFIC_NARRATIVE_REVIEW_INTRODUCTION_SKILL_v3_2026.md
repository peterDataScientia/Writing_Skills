# Scientific Narrative Review Introduction Writing Skill — 2026 Evidence Edition

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document extends, but does **not overwrite**, `SCIENTIFIC_INTRODUCTION_SKILL.md` (Version 2.0.0). It specializes that general scientific-writing guidance for **critical narrative reviews**. It draws on ten open-access biomedical review articles published in **2026**. All ten article identities, publisher labels, open-access status, and publication dates were checked against their publisher records. Relevant Introduction passages were examined through publisher pages or searchable full-text records. The precise extent of text available varies by article, so the individual observations below are qualitative examples, **not a sentence-by-sentence formal content analysis**.

**Important classification distinction:** The ten publishers classify these papers as `Review` or `Review Article`; that label does not establish a systematic methodology, nor does it mean each author formally calls the work a *critical narrative review*. Item 7 explicitly describes its approach as a critical narrative review. Do not describe the entire corpus as ten papers that self-identify as critical narrative reviews. Avoid claiming that their journals' current impact factors or quartiles were independently audited here; the journals were selected for prominence and biomedical relevance.

**Guiding principle:** The Introduction must establish an intelligible scientific **problem and interpretive rationale** for a review, not merely present disease background or a catalogue of chemotypes. Its depth and length must be driven by argument, not a fixed paragraph count.

### 2.1 Individual Introduction audits

**R1 — ADC engineering: interdependent constraints rather than a single success metric.**
- **Entry:** Define the therapeutic modality and how it is supposed to work.
- **Argument:** Effective delivery depends on antigen binding, trafficking, linker/payload behavior, and the failure modes of those steps. Molecular engineering and AI cannot be judged using one property alone.
- **Exit:** Scope spans design components, physics-based approaches, prediction, safety, and validation.
- **Reusable lesson:** Map dependencies among evidence domains, not merely list technical methods.
- **Avoid imitating:** Extensive prospective claims for AI without matching experimental validation.

**R2 — Oncogenic NSCLC: clinical advance followed by unresolved management problems.**
- **Entry:** Disease burden, actionable mutations, treatment advances.
- **Argument:** Despite improved targeted therapies, resistance and central nervous system progression create concrete therapeutic decisions that remain difficult.
- **Exit:** Clear scope for mutation classes, outcomes, resistance, and CNS progression.
- **Reusable lesson:** Success-plus-limitation can motivate a review more effectively than generic statements that the field is growing.
- **Avoid imitating:** Broad descriptions of therapy that outrun the precise objective.

**R3 — Cancer resistance: begin with the bottleneck.**
- **Entry:** Resistance and relapse are named immediately as the problem.
- **Argument:** Distinguishes intrinsic from acquired resistance, tumor-intrinsic from extrinsic processes, and highlights emerging analytical technologies.
- **Exit:** Explains how the review organizes mechanisms and potential interventions across malignancies.
- **Reusable lesson:** A review can start directly with a clinically meaningful unresolved process rather than general textbook background.
- **Avoid imitating:** Catalogues of pathways, cancer types, or technologies without a comparison principle.

**R4 — Targeting cancer stem cells: separate mechanistic plausibility from clinical translation.**
- **Entry:** CSC properties and disease relevance.
- **Argument:** Preclinical therapeutic rationale is challenged by heterogeneity, changing cell states, and biomarker and trial-design difficulties.
- **Exit:** Reviews obstacles and means to connect laboratory models to patient-relevant assessments.
- **Reusable lesson:** An attractive target does not automatically produce an actionable or clinically validated intervention.
- **Avoid imitating:** Treating disease relevance as proof of target-specific therapeutic efficacy.

**R5 — Adaptive immunotherapy resistance: a discriminating conceptual framework.**
- **Entry:** Immunotherapy progress coupled with limited durable responses.
- **Argument:** Separates failure of immediate effector function from failure of durable immune memory, explaining why they can produce different clinical outcomes.
- **Exit:** States the mechanism-centered framework organizing the review.
- **Reusable lesson:** A compelling critical narrative review may introduce an evidence-based distinction that changes how existing observations are interpreted.
- **Avoid imitating:** Turning a proposed organizing framework into an experimentally established universal mechanism.

**R6 — Macrophages: revise an oversimplified biological classification.**
- **Entry:** Core macrophage functions and disease biology.
- **Argument:** Contrasts the traditional M1/M2 dichotomy with evidence for functional, spatial, metabolic, and ontogenetic heterogeneity.
- **Exit:** Introduces a regulatory view and a therapeutic interpretation of plasticity.
- **Reusable lesson:** Identify the limits of a common simplifying model, then explain what newer evidence can support.
- **Avoid imitating:** Adding many mechanistic layers without stating their distinct contributions to the central question.

**R7 — AI in oncology: claim-matched evidence in a critical narrative review.**
- **Entry:** Heterogeneity of cancer and of AI-enabled tasks.
- **Argument:** Separates prediction, correlation, mechanism, workflow usefulness, and improved patient outcomes. A high predictive metric cannot by itself establish clinical utility.
- **Exit:** Poses a sharp evaluative question about which scientific claims different types of evidence permit.
- **Special methodological point:** The article separately describes a **critical narrative review** design in a Methods section. This is one acceptable form of transparency, not a universal requirement.
- **Reusable lesson:** The strongest transferable model for HPSE is **claim-to-evidence matching**: an assay IC50, a cellular phenotype, a target-engagement measure, and clinical efficacy answer different questions.
- **Avoid imitating:** Imposing the same review methods or evidence-map size on an unrelated review without justification.

**R8 — Metastasis: mechanism-to-bottleneck synthesis.**
- **Entry:** Persistent clinical problems despite therapeutic progress.
- **Argument:** Links metastatic stages and adaptive programs to distinct modes of treatment failure.
- **Exit:** States the scope and the intended organizing principles for treatment vulnerabilities.
- **Reusable lesson:** Connect mechanisms to the *specific* failure or uncertainty they help explain; do not present a detached mechanisms inventory.
- **Avoid imitating:** Over-extending the Introduction into a glossary of all disease states unless needed to interpret the review.

**R9 — B cells and cancer: neglected comparator lens.**
- **Entry:** Tumor immune infiltration, the role of B cells relative to more extensively studied T cells.
- **Argument:** Existing attention has been uneven across immune components; B-cell heterogeneity and antibody functions call for synthesis.
- **Exit:** Explains how the review addresses B-cell states, immune effects, and potential therapy/stratification.
- **Reusable lesson:** Place the selected subject against an explicit comparator or dominant prior perspective.
- **Avoid imitating:** Claiming that a previously understudied area has no evidence at all.

**R10 — B-cell functions: context-dependent biological effects.**
- **Entry:** Tumor microenvironment and immune-cell composition.
- **Argument:** B-cell functions can differ across disease contexts, and their heterogeneity complicates uniform interpretation.
- **Exit:** Defines the range of mechanisms and interventions to be discussed.
- **Reusable lesson:** Make context dependence explicit whenever measured biology can have divergent implications.
- **Avoid imitating:** Excessive general microenvironment background before narrowing to the review question.

## 3. Cross-paper synthesis: evidence-informed rules

These are **observed useful patterns**, not mandatory editorial rules imposed by the journals.

1. **Choose a scientifically meaningful entry point.** Biological mechanism, clinical failure, or an interpretive contradiction may each be appropriate. Start as close to the core question as the intended readership permits.
2. **Show the limitation of existing knowledge, not just its quantity.** A substantive gap can be assay comparability, discordant biological effects, confounded mechanisms, poor pharmacokinetic coverage, weak validation, or failure to translate into clinical benefit.
3. **Connect the problem to specific bodies of evidence.** The rationale should follow from findings, not from empty assertions that more research is needed.
4. **Differentiate evidence levels.** Binding, inhibition, selectivity, cell response, tissue engagement, exposure, and patient benefit are not interchangeable endpoints.
5. **Distinguish a review's contribution from its list of topics.** The distinctive contribution may be critical comparison, new framing, reconciliation of conflicting observations, or a claim-matched framework; it need not be a claim that no previous review exists.
6. **End with an accurate purpose and scope.** The final paragraph should say what will be critically evaluated and what comparison the review permits.
7. **Do not force search methods into the Introduction.** A concise transparent description is acceptable for a narrative review. A separate Methods section is also acceptable where appropriate. Do not invent databases, cut-off dates, reviewer counts, or eligibility criteria.
8. **Do not enforce a paragraph template.** Four coherent paragraphs can be better than six padded paragraphs. Length is a decision about argument completeness and journal norms.
9. **Use references to support precise statements.** Cite original studies for specific experiments and prior reviews when positioning a synthesis. Do not treat citation density as a quality score.
10. **Borrow critical discipline, not promotional rhetoric.** High-profile reviews may still contain vague superlatives, long inventories, and underqualified predictions.

## 4. Distinguish types of review: routing rule

| Manuscript | Expected Introduction | Do **not** impose |
|---|---|---|
| Critical narrative review | Define a disputed interpretive question, compare evidence domains, explain significance of uncertainty, and delimit evaluative scope | PRISMA flowcharts, systematic inclusion/exclusion machinery, meta-analysis without design warrant |
| Broad narrative overview | Define field, major advances and challenges, reader need, and coverage | A forced novel mechanistic hypothesis |
| Systematic review | Define systematic question, rationale, and connection to prespecified evidence-synthesis design | Informal selective sampling presented as exhaustive |
| Original experimental article | Identify precise unresolved testable question and how the actual study tests it | A narrative review's broad topic coverage as the objective |

Narrative review does **not** mean absence of transparency. Describe the search approach truthfully, but do not make it appear systematic merely to satisfy a generic template.

## 5. Prewriting evidence map (mandatory in this workflow)

Before editing a manuscript Introduction, prepare the following working matrix:

| Scientific claim | Primary support (authors/DOI) | Evidence level | Uncertainty or alternative explanation | Why it matters | Paragraph role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example: enzyme is inhibited by compound X | Original enzyme-assay study | Biochemical | Substrate- and assay-dependence | Potency cannot be compared freely | Establish interpretive limit |
| Example: exposure reduces invasion | Original cell study | Cellular phenotype | HPSE dependence may be untested | Cell activity is not proof of specific target engagement | Introduce mechanism ambiguity |
| Example: response occurs in treated patients | Trial report | Clinical outcome | Co-therapy or absent pharmacodynamic evidence | Response may not establish HPSE-specific causality | Motivate critical appraisal |

Mark missing original articles `[SOURCE CHECK REQUIRED]`. Never invent support.

## 6. Argument architecture (choose based on evidence)

A useful **four-paragraph architecture** for a concise critical narrative review is:

- **P1 — Biological or pharmacological foundation:** Only the mechanisms needed to understand the review's core evaluation.
- **P2 — Scientific and translational tension:** What has been achieved, and what remains uncertain or hard to attribute causally.
- **P3 — Literature positioning and interpretive gap:** Why accumulated evidence cannot simply be ranked or pooled; how this appraisal complements preceding reviews.
- **P4 — Critical objective and scope:** What will be assessed, across which chemotypes or evidence types, with a truthful brief account of literature selection if useful.

Alternative openings are allowed. For example, R3 starts with the bottleneck, R6 foregrounds limitations of a conventional model, and R7 focuses on what kinds of claims the evidence supports. **The number four is an option, not a rule.**

## 7. Application to the heparanase (HPSE1) review

**Working manuscript title:** *From Biochemical Hits to Pharmacologically Credible Leads: A Critical Appraisal of Heparanase Inhibitor Discovery*.

**Manuscript type:** Critical narrative review, **not** systematic or scoping review.

### 7.1 Recommended single-sentence argument map

1. HPSE1-mediated heparan sulfate remodeling creates a biochemically tractable but context-dependent target.
2. Diverse inhibitor classes have biochemical and disease-model findings, while target-specific causal attribution and clinical translation remain difficult.
3. Assay heterogeneity and incomplete connections among SAR, mechanism, selectivity, target engagement and exposure limit the interpretation of apparent lead quality.
4. The review critically compares those evidence domains across small-molecule programs and glycomimetic benchmarks, identifying which claims are experimentally justified.

### 7.2 Distinct claims requiring separate evidence

- **Catalysis and substrate recognition:** Structural and substrate-specificity studies, including the relevant HPSE active-site residues.
- **Measured inhibition:** Specific substrate/readout, enzyme form, experimental conditions, potency metric, and controls.
- **Chemistry:** Within-series matched-pair SAR is not a transferable cross-scaffold potency hierarchy.
- **Selectivity:** Relevant enzyme-family and interaction panels must reflect the inhibitor modality.
- **Target engagement:** Phenotypic changes are not equivalent to direct HPSE-dependent engagement.
- **Pharmacology:** Exposure and pharmacodynamic time course are different from biological efficacy endpoints.
- **Human outcomes:** Combination responses and trial outcomes may not isolate an HPSE-specific contribution.

### 7.3 What to preserve in the user's current Introduction

- Existing **four-paragraph** biological-to-translational progression.
- Scientific caution (e.g., association vs causation and biochemical vs phenotypic evidence).
- Honest acknowledgment of the Zhang and Cui small-molecule review rather than claiming unverified first-review status.
- Small molecules as primary focus; glycomimetics as comparators.
- A concise critical objective rather than a long topic inventory.
- Removal of the exact search-update date is permissible **if** the remaining search disclosure is accurate and journal rules do not require it. Do not independently reinsert the date.

### 7.4 Minimal revisions worth considering

- Use one strong early statement of the biochemical-hit-to-pharmacological-lead gap.
- Ensure the prior-review comparison identifies the **interpretive** contribution, not only additional areas covered.
- Reduce overlapping claims about potency, target engagement, and exposure; each should appear where it advances the argument.
- Do not add filler biological background or force a fifth/sixth paragraph.
- Confirm that citations adjacent to the mechanism examples actually support those examples. Publication year alone is not a citation audit.

## 8. Sentence-level editorial checklist

For **every sentence**, ask:

1. What is the precise claim, and which original publication supports it?
2. Is it established knowledge, an observation, an interpretation, or a hypothesis?
3. Why does the Introduction need this claim rather than a later section?
4. Does it add a new distinction, advance a causal argument, or make a necessary transition?
5. Has the sentence conflated biochemical activity with clinical pharmacology?
6. Does the citation match the specific clause that precedes it?
7. Can an adjective or a list be removed without losing information?
8. Is the degree of certainty appropriate, especially for pathway mechanisms and clinical interpretation?
9. Is an acronym introduced unnecessarily or before it is needed?
10. Does it lead logically to the next sentence and paragraph?

Avoid first-person plural (`we`, `our`, `us`), vague novelty declarations, defensive author language, teaching-style definitions, excessive methods inventory, and layout hyphenation artifacts.

## 9. Editorial quality rubric (100 points)

| Dimension | Maximum |
|---|---:|
| Biological/therapeutic relevance without textbook padding | 10 |
| Accuracy and synthesis of prior evidence | 20 |
| Scientific tension / specific interpretive gap | 20 |
| Distinctive but bounded review rationale | 15 |
| Paragraph-to-paragraph and sentence-level coherence | 15 |
| Citation relevance and factual integrity | 15 |
| Precision, concision, restraint | 5 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Use scores only when sources are actually checked. A fabricated citation, invented literature-search method, or unsupported novelty claim requires correction regardless of aggregate score. Do not equate the score with journal acceptance probability.

## 10. Editing workflow and output contract

1. **Read** the submitted Introduction plus the abstract, principal sections, conclusions, and current reference list.
2. **Identify** manuscript type and target journal, if specified.
3. **Build** a claim-to-evidence map that distinguishes primary findings from review-level synthesis.
4. **Audit** introduction-to-body consistency: do later sections actually establish the cited mechanisms, selectivity, PK/PD, and clinical interpretation?
5. **Review** at least several directly relevant field-specific original papers; treat the ten 2026 reviews as writing exemplars, not as factual substitutes for HPSE primary sources.
6. **Develop** a paragraph argument map; preserve the author's current structure if it works.
7. **Revise selectively**: minimize scope drift, redundant wording, and unsupported certainty.
8. **Verify** in-text citations and the truthfulness of any database/date/methodology description.
9. **Present** (a) the revised Introduction, (b) a concise change log, (c) a citation audit specifying checked versus unchecked sources, and (d) unresolved verification tasks.
10. **Do not** call text publication-ready until a target-journal and source-level audit is complete.

## 11. Limits of this training document

- This is a reusable authoring guide derived from **a purposive set of ten 2026 reviews**, not a systematic meta-study of scientific writing.
- Article classifications and dates were verified on publishers' pages; individual introductions were reviewed through accessible full text/searchable passages. Qualitative lessons should not be reported as quantified frequencies, and partial-access observations should not be over-interpreted.
- It is **not** legitimate to infer universal high-impact writing standards from ten articles, or to attribute journal editorial requirements to examples of published prose.
- Do not reproduce substantial verbatim Introduction passages; links and paraphrased writing observations are sufficient.
- Do not mix the 2026 review corpus with the earlier **original-research** training corpus when calculating any narrative-review-specific trends.
