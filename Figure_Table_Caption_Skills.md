# Publication-Grade Figure and Table Caption Skills

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Scope:** Scientific manuscripts, especially systematic reviews and quantitative evidence syntheses  
**Use:** Editorial rules and an audit checklist, not a replacement for journal instructions or source-data verification.

## 1. Governing principle

A caption identifies what a display contains and supplies only the information necessary to interpret it accurately. It is **not** a second Results paragraph, an abstract, an account of how the review was conducted, or a claim of scientific significance. A table title serves the same identifying function. Technical qualifications go in a *Note* below a table or figure when needed.

Do not use long generic introductions such as “This figure illustrates…” or editorial claims such as “clearly demonstrates,” “remarkable,” “highly significant,” “novel,” or “important insight.” Describe the measured comparison, unit, grouping, statistic, and visual encoding precisely.

**Distinguish styles:** APA 7 uses a bold standalone figure/table number followed by an italicized, title-case title above the display and an optional note below. Many scientific journals instead place a single sentence-style caption below a figure. Always follow the target journal's instructions. Do not mix APA conventions mechanically with a journal's production style.

## 2. Evidence-based standards

1. **PRISMA 2020:** Flow diagrams should make the counts and stages of identification, screening, retrieval, eligibility and inclusion inspectable. Accurate source accounting belongs **in the diagram and Results text**, not unnecessarily repeated in its caption. A figure caption cannot repair inaccurate PRISMA counts.
2. **APA 7:** Number, concise descriptive title, body/image, and optional note. In tables, general notes explain abbreviations and conventions; specific notes address individual cells; probability notes explain statistical symbols. Use notes only when needed.
3. **Cochrane:** Display captions and titles should be complete, descriptive and oriented to the outcome or analysis. Forest plots are appropriate when their underlying estimates and uncertainty warrant them; do not label a dot plot as a forest plot unless it really represents forest-plot estimates and intervals.
4. **Scientific integrity:** Captions must match data transformations, grouping variables, axes, reference lines, population/study denominators, error bars and analysis unit. Do not infer what a mark means from appearance alone; verify against plotting code and analysis source.

## 3. Caption construction procedure

**A. Identify the object.** Flow diagram? Paired scatterplot? Study-level ratio plot? Leave-one-study-out plot? Within-study point distribution? Mechanism schematic? Each deserves its own description.

**B. Write the shortest accurate title.** Prefer a substantive noun phrase: “Paired inhibitory concentrations against jack bean and *Helicobacter pylori* ureases” rather than “Analysis of results.” Use terms like *ratio*, *concentration*, *geometric mean* or *sensitivity analysis* only when the plotted measure justifies them.

**C. Audit the picture.** Check axes, units, log scales, colors, legends, numbers, confidence intervals, line types, ordering, panels and sample sizes against the actual plotted file.

**D. Put indispensable decoding in a note.** Examples: “Each point represents one paired measurement”; “The dashed line denotes equal inhibitory concentrations”; “Values above 1 indicate a higher HPU/JBU inhibitory concentration ratio.” Avoid repeating material already perfectly clear from the axis or legend.

**E. Remove interpretation.** Statements about robustness, agreement, transferability, superiority, influence, bias, or why the result matters generally belong in Results/Discussion, supported by analysis.

**F. Cross-check the manuscript.** The first text callout must precede or accompany the display; figure and table numbering must match; numbers must agree with the source dataset and methods.

## 4. Figure caption patterns and examples

**PRISMA flow diagram**

> **Figure 1.** PRISMA 2020 flow diagram of study selection.

A longer variant is appropriate only if it adds real distinction, e.g. identification from multiple information sources. Report source-specific counts in the identification boxes, and category-specific full-text exclusions only when supported by the audit ledger.

**Paired scatterplot**

> **Figure 2.** Paired inhibitory concentrations against jack bean and *Helicobacter pylori* ureases. *Note.* Each point represents a paired observation from the same compound and source study. The dashed diagonal denotes equality between the two measurements. Axes use logarithmic scales.

Retain the final sentence only if the actual plotting axes are logarithmic. Verify that symbols and legend distinguish the stated groups.

**Study-level geometric ratios**

> **Figure 3.** Study-specific geometric mean ratios of *H. pylori* to jack bean urease inhibitory concentrations. *Note.* Each point summarizes the paired observations within the indicated study. The vertical reference at a ratio of 1 indicates equality of the two concentrations.

Check whether the reference line is vertical or horizontal, given the chart orientation. If study-level estimates have intervals, name them precisely rather than inventing a confidence level.

**Leave-one-study-out sensitivity analysis**

> **Figure 4.** Leave-one-study-out estimates of the geometric mean HPU/JBU inhibitory concentration ratio. *Note.* Each estimate is recalculated after excluding the named study. Reference lines indicate the full-dataset estimate and a ratio of 1.

Do not call this a forest plot merely because it has horizontal markers. Specify whether the pooled estimator is compound-weighted or equal-study-weighted; the title/note must use the estimator actually plotted.

**Within-study distribution**

> **Figure 5.** Within-study distribution of log₁₀-transformed HPU/JBU inhibitory concentration ratios. *Note.* Points represent individual paired observations. Short ticks identify study-level means for groups with multiple observations.

Verify whether ticks are arithmetic means of *log* ratios or geometric means expressed on a log scale. Do not describe the latter incorrectly.

### Figure caption rules

- State what is plotted, not what the reader should conclude.
- Distinguish *number of studies* from *number of pairs*, *records* and *reports*.
- Define unfamiliar abbreviations at first use in title or note; use terms consistently thereafter.
- Use the correct chemical/biological notation, subscript and symbols (e.g., IC₅₀ vs study-defined I₅₀). Do **not** silently relabel source-defined endpoints.
- If a display's colors and symbols are already explicitly keyed within its image, do not transcribe the full legend into the caption.
- Describe uncertainty precisely: SD, SE, 95% CI, bootstrap percentile CI, or range are not interchangeable.
- If adapted or reproduced from another source, provide appropriate attribution and check permissions.
- Keep figures interpretable in grayscale or for viewers with color-vision differences; ensure adequate text contrast and export quality.

## 5. Table titles and notes

The table title tells the reader the scope of the table. The table body contains the actual numbers and categorical data. Notes document qualifications, not the paper's argument.

**APA 7 default display layout**

**Table 1**  
*Characteristics of Studies Included in the Review*

*Note.* JBU = jack bean urease; HPU = *Helicobacter pylori* urease. Values and assay conditions are as reported by the original authors unless a stated transformation was applied.

**Table 2**  
*Paired Measurements of Urease Inhibitory Potency*

*Note.* Concentrations are accompanied by their units. Source-reported I₅₀ and IC₅₀ labels are distinguished where applicable; conversions must be documented rather than implied. Do not claim all reported values are the same type of endpoint unless the source studies support that statement.

**Table 3**  
*Study-Level HPU/JBU Inhibitory Concentration Ratios*

*Note.* Ratios were calculated from paired measurements according to the estimator specified in Methods. A ratio of 1 indicates equal inhibitory concentrations; a ratio below 1 indicates a lower reported concentration against HPU than against JBU. This does not by itself establish biological transferability.

These are **draft patterns**, not confirmed titles for existing manuscript tables. Recheck the actual table columns and methods before insertion. If Table 3 instead contains leave-one-study-out results, retitle accordingly.

### APA table construction checks

- Table numbers increase in the order of first mention; number and title go above the table.
- Use a concise, italicized descriptive title in title case under APA 7, unless a target journal requires another style.
- Column headers are informative and units appear in headers or cells as appropriate.
- Use uniform unit and significant-digit conventions within a column; indicate exceptions explicitly.
- Avoid vertical rules; use minimal horizontal rules where needed for structure.
- Explain abbreviations, superscripts, symbols, special cases and missing-value codes.
- Distinguish “not reported,” “not measured,” “not eligible,” and “not calculable.” These are not synonyms.
- A general *Note.* comes before superscript-specific notes and any probability notes.
- Do not duplicate the Discussion or repeat the whole Methods in footnotes.
- Tables must remain machine-readable and editable when the manuscript requires editable text.

## 6. Relationship among abstract, Results, captions and Discussion

| Location | Proper content | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract | Question, eligible evidence, synthesis method, key results, calibrated conclusion | Detailed database counts and long caption-like explanations |
| Study Selection subsection | Verified search/accounting results with relevant numbers | Duplicating every box verbatim |
| Figure caption | Display identification and essential legend/encoding | Interpretation, speculation and unnecessary numeric recital |
| Table title/notes | Dataset scope, headings, units and technical clarifications | Novelty and mechanistic claims |
| Results prose | Precise numerical findings and comparisons supported by figures/tables | Repeating captions |
| Discussion | Interpretation, limitations, consequences and relation to established evidence | Introducing unsupported analyses |

## 7. Urease review: fixed analysis details that control wording

The latest discussed review contains **12 studies** and **45 paired observations**. The study-level distribution is highly uneven: Odake et al. (1994) contributes **29 of 45** pairs. The prior analysis reported Spearman ρ ≈ **0.792**, a compound-weighted geometric HPU/JBU ratio ≈ **0.512**, and an equal-study ratio ≈ **1.369**. These values guide factual checking; **do not automatically insert them into captions**. Their use in the Results must be reconciled with the currently approved statistical workbook and exact estimator definitions.

PRISMA identification figures previously reconciled for the review were PubMed **1,362**, Scopus **87**, Google Scholar **31**, and Semantic Scholar **2,148**, totaling **3,628** hits before deduplication. The amended flow has **1,490** duplicate removals, **2,138** screened records, **2,022** screening exclusions, **116** reports assessed, **104** full-text exclusions, and **12** included studies. These counts are a *working reconciled flow*; older audit files warned of unresolved deduplication/screening provenance. Do not falsely claim completion of a full provenance audit merely because arithmetic balances. Categories of the 104 full-text exclusions must come from the verified exclusion log, not be invented.

If the exact manuscript figures are numbered differently, update the example numbering **from the manuscript**, not by assumption. A template is not evidence that a figure exists.

## 8. Mandatory pre-submission caption audit

- [ ] Have I viewed the actual final figure or table, not only its filename?
- [ ] Does the caption accurately name the comparison and plotted statistic?
- [ ] Do the title, note, axes, units and legend agree?
- [ ] Are log transformations and reference lines accurately explained?
- [ ] Have I distinguished observations, records, reports and studies?
- [ ] Are all numbers and citation/year labels supported by the audited source?
- [ ] Do notes specify actual uncertainty measures rather than guessing?
- [ ] Is the caption concise, grammatically complete and free of Results interpretation?
- [ ] Are titles and notes consistent with the target journal's required format?
- [ ] Are figure/table numbers and citations consistent with body text?
- [ ] Have I verified source-specific measurement names including I₅₀ vs IC₅₀?
- [ ] Have I excluded fabricated search sources or reasons from PRISMA?
- [ ] Are tables editable, accessible, and free of unnecessary rules?
- [ ] Has the final manuscript been rendered and pages checked for caption detachment or overlap?

## 9. Editorial rule for future revisions

Before rewriting any abstract, figure caption or table title: **read its underlying evidence and the current manuscript context**; identify which statements are supported, which are inference, and which remain uncertain. Do not overwrite existing approved content or rename the working manuscript file without authorization. Provide the proposed wording for review when the user has asked to approve editorial changes first.
